# Minuri — Guide Journey Personas

Six selectable journeys for newcomers to Melbourne. Each persona represents a distinct arrival situation. Users pick the one that matches their reality; the app surfaces a curated sequence of guides tailored to that path.

---

## Persona 1 — Mei, 21 · International Student

**Origin:** Shanghai, China  
**Situation:** First-ever time living alone. Arrived two weeks before semester starts with a suitcase, a student visa, and a list of things she was told to "just figure out when you get there." She has a rough idea of what university life should look like from films and her older cousins' Instagram posts — but the reality of Melbourne is quieter, more confusing, and lonelier than she expected. She doesn't know what Medicare is. She's been eating instant noodles because she walked into Woolworths and froze at the unfamiliar brands. She misses her mum's cooking in a way that feels embarrassing to admit.

Her stress isn't dramatic — it's the slow accumulation of small things she doesn't know how to do. She's capable and organised; she just lacks the local context that everyone around her seems to take for granted.

**Tagline:** "I left everything familiar. Where do I even start?"

## Persona 2 — Daniel, 25 · Interstate Graduate

**Origin:** Brisbane, Queensland  
**Situation:** Landed a junior role at a Melbourne design studio — his first real job. He's lived out of home since 18, paid his own bills, cooked his own meals. By his own measure, he's independent. But Melbourne is a different city with different rhythms, and his whole social infrastructure — the friends, the local spots, the weekend plans — is still back in Brisbane. He moved four weeks before starting work to give himself time to "settle in," and those four weeks have been the most aimless of his adult life. He doesn't know which suburbs are worth exploring, why his Myki keeps running out, or how to make friends in a city where everyone already has their people. He keeps getting on the wrong tram.

He doesn't need hand-holding. He needs orientation.

**Tagline:** "I got the job. Now I need to get a life here."

## Persona 3 — Priya, 34 · Skilled Migrant

**Origin:** Bangalore, India  
**Situation:** Arrived with her partner and their two-year-old daughter on a skilled visa. She starts a product manager role in three weeks; her partner is job-hunting. They have savings, a temporary rental sorted through an agent, and a genuine will to build a life here — but everything administrative feels like a wall. Which GP can see a toddler without a $200 gap fee? What happens if the lease agent keeps the bond? What does "urgent repair" mean legally? Is their overseas health insurance valid, or do they need to enrol somewhere? The questions stack up faster than answers arrive.

She's not overwhelmed by independence — she's managed a household and a career simultaneously for years. What overwhelms her is the specific, local, procedural knowledge that Australia assumes you arrive with.

**Tagline:** "My family needs stability. I need to figure out how everything works here."

## Persona 4 — Sam, 23 · Working Holiday Maker

**Origin:** Bristol, United Kingdom  
**Situation:** Here for 12 months on a Working Holiday visa. No long-term plan, which is the point. He's done a bit of research, has $4,000 AUD saved, and is splitting a share house in Fitzroy with people he met on a Facebook group. He's not anxious about Melbourne — he's excited. What he needs isn't reassurance, it's efficiency: how to get around without accidentally spending $20/day on transport, where to eat well for under $10, which trails are worth cycling. He wants to explore as much as possible before his money runs out or his visa expires, whichever comes first.

His risk is not systems-confusion but quiet financial erosion — small daily costs that compound into a shortened trip.

**Tagline:** "I'm here for the adventure. Just need to not run out of money."

## Persona 5 — Aiko, 28 · Humanitarian Arrival

**Origin:** Yangon, Myanmar  
**Situation:** Arrived three months ago through a humanitarian visa pathway. Her English is functional but effortful. She has a caseworker through a settlement service who helped with housing, but the ongoing questions — where to get food when money runs out before the next payment, what to do when she feels like she can't get out of bed, who to call at 2am when the panic is loud — those she's navigating alone. She doesn't always know which services are free. She sometimes doesn't ask because she's been told no before in ways that felt humiliating. She is more resilient than most people who've never been tested, and she is exhausted in ways that are hard to describe.

She doesn't need motivation. She needs to know exactly what exists, what it costs (if anything), and that it's genuinely available to her.

**Tagline:** "I made it here. Now I need to find my footing."

## Persona 6 — Marcus, 41 · Professional Expat

**Origin:** London, United Kingdom  
**Situation:** Relocated for a senior operations role at a Melbourne firm. His salary is solid, his accommodation is sorted, and his employer ran an orientation session that covered where to get a Myki card. That was it. He now has a functional flat and no idea how Australian superannuation works, whether his lease is standard or exploitative, what the difference between a bulk-billing and a private GP is, or why he keeps running into people who seem to know each other already. He moved without his London social circle and has discovered that professional competence does not transfer into social ease in a new city. He books meetings more easily than he makes friends.

He doesn't need the beginner basics — he needs the Australian-specific layer underneath the familiar surface.

**Tagline:** "I'm senior at work. But here I don't know the basics."

## Design Notes

- **Card UI:** Name + age + origin chip, illustrated character against maze/city background, short tagline
- **Selection flow:** pick persona → preview journey steps → "Follow this journey" CTA
- **Arc badges:** Day 1 / Week 1 / Month 1 shown as progress steps on journey path
- **Overlap intentional:** same guide appears across multiple personas; different entry points, different context
- **Tone across all:** no pity, no infantilising. Every persona is capable — they're missing local knowledge, not ability
