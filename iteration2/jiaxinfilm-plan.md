# Minuri — Learning Journey Film Plan

**Format:** Timelapse + Narrative Montage
**Target Runtime:** ~5 minutes
**Audience:** Internal (Team TP39)

---

## What We're Going For

Each person films themselves working on their part. These segments are edited together with transitions so one person's work flows into the next. At the end, everything comes together into a single view of the finished product — showing how all the individual pieces built up to it.

---

## Structure at a Glance

| Act | Section | Timestamp |
|-----|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Cold Open — Camera opens, first concept sketch | 0:00 – 0:30 |
| 2 | Each Team Member | 0:30 – 2:30 |
| 3 | Integration Montage | 2:30 – 3:30 |
| 4 | Product Reveal | 3:30 – 4:20 |
| 5 | Reflections + Outro | 4:20 – 5:00 |

## Act 1 — Cold Open (0:00–0:30)

The video opens with a single person reaching over and opening their camera to start recording — the deliberate decision to document the journey. Then we see the very first concept of Minuri being drawn.

**What to include:**
- Shot of a hand reaching toward the camera and pressing record — filmed from slightly to the side so you can see both the person and the camera
- Cut to them settling in front of a blank piece of paper (or whiteboard)
- A short timelapse of the first concept sketch being drawn — rough shapes, boxes, arrows, whatever the original looked like
- Hold on the finished sketch for a moment before the music gently fades in
- The tagline appears over the sketch: *"Still feeling home, wherever you are."*
- Keep sound natural here — pen on paper, ambient room noise, no music until the tagline

**Important:** If the original sketch still exists, use it. If not, recreate the session honestly — film it as a "this is where it started" throwback rather than pretending it's the real thing. Either works.

> This opening works because the audience sees the *decision* to begin, not just the work itself. It makes everything that follows feel intentional.

---

## Act 2 — Each Team Member (0:30–2:30)

This is the main section. Each person gets around **20–25 seconds** showing what they worked on.

### Format per person

1. A quick candid shot — face or hands, 2–3 seconds, nothing posed
2. A timelapse of their screen showing real work in progress
3. A transition into the next person

### Suggested order

| Order | Focus Area | What the Timelapse Should Show |
|-------|-----------|-------------------------------|
| 1st | Research / UX | Figma wireframes building, persona cards |
| 2nd | UI Design | Colour palette, components being arranged |
| 3rd | Frontend | Code editor, React components rendering |
| 4th | Data / Backend | API calls, map pins loading, datasets |
| 5th | Full-stack / Lead | Figma and VS Code side by side, integrating pieces |

### Transition options between people

Pick one style and keep it consistent throughout:

- **Screen wipe** — one person's window slides off, the next slides in
- **Match cut** — a Figma frame becomes a live browser component
- **Zoom in** — zoom into the screen until it fills the frame, then zoom out to reveal the next person
- **Cursor handoff** — one cursor exits right, a new one enters from the left

---

## Act 3 — Integration Montage (2:30–3:30)

This is where the pace picks up. Show everything coming together.

**How it kicks off — the WhatsApp trigger:**
- Someone sends a short message in the team group chat — something like *"let's build this"* or *"integration time"*
- Film each person's phone lighting up with the notification, then setting it down and turning to their laptop
- Edit these back to back — four or five phones buzzing in quick succession
- This is the visual signal that Act 3 has begun

**What to include:**
- Split screen showing 2–3 people working at the same time, then more
- The edit cuts get faster as the music builds — this is intentional
- Key integration moments to capture:
  - A component being merged into main branch
  - A PR being reviewed and approved
  - The app loading with real data for the first time
- Include at least one honest moment — an error, a fix, a small win — to keep it real

> **Note:** Don't over-polish this section. The friction is part of the story.

---

## Act 4 — Product Reveal (3:30–4:20)

After all the build-up, let the product speak for itself.

**What to record:**
- A clean screen recording of Minuri being used, showing:
  - Landing page loading
  - Browsing First Time Guides
  - Near Me map finding a resource
- Cut in candid reaction shots of team members seeing it work — unscripted
- Slow the pace down here after the fast integration section
- End on a held frame of the full product — let it sit for a moment

---

## Act 5 — Reflections + Outro (4:20–5:00)

Short, honest, personal. Each person says one thing on camera — not scripted.

**Prompt suggestions (just to get them talking):**
- *"At the start I had no idea how to…"*
- *"The hardest part was…"*
- *"What I'm proud of is…"*

**Closing sequence:**
- Quick cuts of everyone's one-liners
- Final shot of the full team together
- Fade to black with tagline: *"Still feeling home, wherever you are."*
- Text card: **Minuri | FIT5120 Team TP39 | 2026**

## What Everyone Needs to Record

Each team member is responsible for capturing their own footage. Here's what you need:

- [ ] **Screen timelapse** during a real work session — use OBS, QuickTime, or any screen recorder. Minimum 1080p.
- [ ] **Over-the-shoulder or side shot** of yourself working — natural light is fine, doesn't need to be perfect
- [ ] **One reflection clip** (10–15 seconds) filmed at the end of the project — just talk naturally

Additional footage to grab as a team:
- [ ] **WhatsApp notification shot** — everyone films their phone screen receiving the group message, then turning to their laptop. Keep it short and natural, 3–5 seconds per person.
- [ ] Any team meetings, stand-ups, or Figma review sessions
- [ ] The first live run-through of the complete app — record this, you only get one shot at a genuine first reaction

## Emotional Arc (Keep This in Mind When Editing)

```
Camera opens → First sketch → Individual effort → Friction & progress → Everything converges → Product → Reflection
```

The goal is for it to feel like a story, not a showreel. The messy parts — late sessions, bugs, last-minute fixes — are what make it authentic. Don't cut those out.
